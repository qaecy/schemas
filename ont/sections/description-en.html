The QAECY Ontology: Description back to ToC

The QAECY Ontology defines the core knowledge model used by QAECY and the Cue Agent to represent information from Architecture, Engineering, and Construction projects. The model is centered around information objects and entity references. Information is represented through FileLocation, InformationObject and Fragment, while project meaning is represented through EntityMention and CanonicalEntity. Entity mentions are resolved to canonical entities, which can be qualified with categories, linked to properties, and connected through explicit relationships. Entity-to-entity semantics are organized under qcy:relatedEntity through five top-level relation groups, chosen so that each asserts something different about the pair: qcy:contains (part-whole, transitive), qcy:involves (participation, where the object exists independently of the subject), qcy:follows (order or consequence, transitive, with the later entity as the subject), qcy:expressedBy (anything that is a quality, quantity, time, material or form of the subject) and qcy:interfaces (symmetric; two things that touch, overlap, communicate or work together). Specific intentions - a signature date, an author, an adjacency - are not sub-properties. They are qcy:RelationCategory instances that name the group they qualify through qcy-t:reifies, and are attached to a particular edge through a qcy:RelationMeta node, which also carries a free-text qcy:description of that instance. A fixed vocabulary of predicates never covers the tail; a group plus a note does. Categories are managed through qcy:Category and its sub-types: qcy:EntityCategory, qcy:ContentCategory, qcy:MimeCategory, qcy:ProviderType, and qcy:RelationCategory. These are intended as controlled vocabularies for classification and context, and should be extended by adding new SKOS concepts, typically in the qcy-e namespace or in project-specific enum namespaces. Project-specific specializations are expressed as qcy:RelationCategory instances rather than as new properties, so the set of predicates stays fixed while domain precision grows in the catalogues. A category declares the group it reifies and the entity categories it applies between. The earlier sub-property specializations remain in the ontology as owl:deprecated so that graphs written against them stay readable.
